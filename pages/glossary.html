Glossary

Short definitions of the terms and abbreviations used on this site, each with a link to the page where it matters.

0-9 A B C D E F G H I K M N O P R S T U

Terms A to Z

0-9

2B / 3B (thread class, inch)
Tolerance class for internal Unified threads. 2B is the normal commercial fit; 3B is tighter. External threads use 2A / 3A. Thread callouts
6H / 6g (thread class, metric)
ISO metric tolerance classes. 6H is the standard internal (nut) class, 6g the standard external (bolt) class. Thread callouts

A

Anisotropy
Properties that change with direction. Printed parts, FDM most of all, are weaker across the layers (Z) than along them. 3DP quality tips
Anodize, Type II / Type III
Electrochemical oxide layer on aluminum (MIL-A-8625). Type II is decorative and dyeable, up to about 0.001 in thick. Type III (hardcoat) is thicker and harder and grows up to about 0.003 in on a diameter. Tolerances & finishes

B

Ball end mill
Cutter with a fully rounded tip (radius = half the diameter). Used for 3D surfaces and large fillets; slow because it needs many small passes. End mill types
Bridging
A printed span between two supports with nothing underneath. FDM bridges of about 5-10 mm are reliable; longer spans sag. Overhangs & bridging
Bullnose end mill
Flat end mill with a small radius on its corners. The corner radius becomes the pocket floor fillet, so walls and floor are cut in one pass. Standard for hard metals. Floor fillets

C

Chem film (chromate conversion)
Thin conversion coating on aluminum (MIL-DTL-5541). Corrosion protection and paint base with almost no dimensional change; stays electrically conductive. Finishes
Chip load
Thickness of material each cutting edge removes per revolution (in or mm per tooth). Feed rate = chip load x flutes x RPM. Speed / feed conversions
Coil insert (HeliCoil style)
Diamond-section steel wire coil installed in an oversize STI-tapped hole to give strong, wear-resistant threads in soft materials. Threaded inserts
Corner radius vs floor fillet
Corner radius: the vertical inside corner between two pocket walls, set by the cutter diameter. Floor fillet: the radius where a wall meets the pocket floor, set by the cutter's corner radius. Corners & pockets
Cosmetic thread
CAD feature that marks a hole as threaded (dashed major diameter plus callout) without modeling the helix. The geometry stays a plain pilot hole. Don't model threads
Counterbore
Flat-bottomed, larger-diameter recess at the top of a hole so a socket head cap screw sits flush or below the surface. Counterbore sizes
Countersink
Conical recess for a flat-head screw: 82° for inch screws, 90° for metric. Hole rules

D

Datum
Reference feature (face, axis, hole) that dimensions and tolerances are measured from. One clear datum scheme keeps tolerance stacks short. Setups & orientation
DMLS (direct metal laser sintering)
Metal powder bed fusion: a laser fuses fine metal powder layer by layer. Parts are built on supports and stress relieved. 3DP processes
Dog-bone / T-bone relief
Small circular cut into an inside corner so a square-cornered mating part seats fully. Cheaper than a tiny corner radius. Corner reliefs

E

Escape hole
Opening that lets trapped powder (SLS, MJF, DMLS) or resin (SLA) out of a hollow or internal passage. Use at least two, at opposite ends. Escape holes

F

FDM (fused deposition modeling)
3D printing by extruding melted plastic filament through a nozzle. Cheap and fast; weakest across layers. 3DP processes
Flat end mill
Cutter with a square tip. Fastest for bulk removal and vertical walls, but leaves a sharp floor edge that many rapid services reject in metals. End mill types

G

GD&T (geometric dimensioning and tolerancing)
Symbol language (ASME Y14.5, ISO 1101) for form, orientation, location and runout tolerances relative to datums. Tolerances

H

HDT (heat deflection temperature)
Temperature at which a plastic bar deflects a set amount under a set load. A quick guide to how hot a printed or molded part can run. 3DP materials
Heat-set insert
Knurled brass insert pressed into a plastic hole with a hot tip; the plastic melts and flows into the knurls. Best threads for printed parts. Threaded inserts

I

IPM (inches per minute)
Feed rate unit for machine axes. mm/min in metric. Speed conversions

K

Key-locking insert
Solid threaded insert with keys driven into the parent material to lock it. High pull-out and torque strength; needs a larger hole and counterbore. Threaded inserts

M

Major / minor diameter
Major: the largest diameter of a thread (the nominal size). Minor: the smallest (thread roots of a bolt, crests of a nut). The tap drill is close to the minor diameter. Tap drill charts
MJF (Multi Jet Fusion)
HP powder bed process: an inkjet prints fusing agent on nylon powder and infrared lamps fuse it. Consistent production nylon parts. 3DP processes
MMC / LMC
Maximum / least material condition: the size limit with the most / least material (smallest hole / largest hole). Position tolerances at MMC gain bonus tolerance as the feature departs from MMC. GD&T basics

N

NPT (National Pipe Taper)
Tapered US pipe thread that seals by wedging (with sealant). Model a straight pilot hole; the taper is cut by the tool. NPT table

O

Overhang
Printed surface facing down with nothing below it. About 45° from horizontal self-supports in most processes; flatter needs supports (not in SLS / MJF). Overhang rule

P

Passivation
Acid treatment of stainless steel that removes free iron and thickens the protective oxide (ASTM A967). No dimensional change. Finishes
PBF (powder bed fusion)
Family of 3D printing processes that fuse a powder bed layer by layer: SLS and MJF (plastics), DMLS / SLM (metals). 3DP processes
Percent thread
How much of the full thread depth the tap cuts, set by the tap drill size. 75% is the common default; 50-60% in tough metals taps easier with little strength loss. Percent thread calculator
Pilot hole
The plain hole you model for a threaded feature, at the tap drill or vendor pilot diameter. Quoting software reads its diameter to offer threads. Thread pilot tables
Pitch
Distance between adjacent threads. Metric threads are named by pitch (M6 x 1.0); inch threads by threads per inch (1 / pitch). Metric tap drills

R

Ra (roughness average)
Average surface roughness, in microinches (µin) or micrometres (µm). 125 µin Ra (3.2 µm) is the typical as-machined finish; 63 µin (1.6 µm) is smoother. Surface roughness
Runout
GD&T control of how much a diameter or face wobbles when the part spins on a datum axis. Replaces concentricity, which ASME Y14.5-2018 removed. GD&T basics

S

Setup
One clamping of the part in the machine. Every re-clamp adds fixturing time and a chance for misalignment between features. Setups & orientation
SFM (surface feet per minute)
Cutting speed at the tool edge. RPM = SFM x 3.82 / diameter (in). Speed conversions
SLA (stereolithography)
3D printing by curing liquid photopolymer resin with a laser, layer by layer. Finest detail and smoothest surface. 3DP processes
SLS (selective laser sintering)
Powder bed fusion of nylon with a laser. No supports needed; tough functional parts. 3DP processes
SMM (surface metres per minute)
Metric cutting speed at the tool edge. RPM = SMM x 1000 / (pi x diameter in mm). Speed conversions
Stepover
Sideways distance between adjacent cutter passes. Small stepovers give smoother 3D surfaces but take longer. End mill types
STI (screw thread insert) thread
The oversize thread tapped for a coil insert; the installed insert then gives the nominal thread size. Coil insert tables

T

Tap drill
Drill sized to leave the right amount of material for a tap or thread mill to form the thread (sets the percent thread). Tap drill charts
Teardrop hole
Horizontal printed hole with a pointed (45°) top so it prints without support and does not sag. Horizontal holes
TPI (threads per inch)
Number of threads in one inch of length for Unified threads: 1/4-20 has 20 TPI. Inch tap drills
True position
GD&T location tolerance: a cylindrical zone around the exact (basic) location where the feature axis must lie. GD&T basics

U

UNC (Unified National Coarse)
Default coarse inch thread series, e.g. 1/4-20. Easiest to tap, most forgiving. Inch tap drills
UNEF (Unified National Extra Fine)
Extra fine inch series (e.g. 1/4-32) for thin walls and fine adjustment. Often needs non-standard tools. Inch tap drills
UNF (Unified National Fine)
Fine inch thread series, e.g. 1/4-28. Better for thin walls, adjustment and vibration. Inch tap drills
Definitions are short working descriptions for design use, not standard text. See Sources & References.
